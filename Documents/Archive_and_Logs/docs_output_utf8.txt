Project Details
1.1
Project Objectives
You are required to extend the work initiated in Assignment #3 and complete
it as a full research project in the form of a technical research paper and imple-
mentation. Students proposing an innovative method must clearly define their
contribution, justify their design choices, and compare their approach with rele-
vant baseline methods. Students not proposing a new method must implement
multiple generative models relevant to their selected problem and perform a
comprehensive comparative analysis. In all cases, the work must demonstrate
strong experimental rigor.
This includes presenting results through training
and validation plots against epochs, error curves, quantitative comparison ta-
bles, and visual outputs where applicable. The goal is to ensure that your work
reflects a complete experimental study with clear insights and analysis.
Projects may fall into one of two categories:
ò 1. Research-Oriented Projects: These should demonstrate innovation
in model design, data processing, or application.
Clearly describe the
novelty introduced and how it has been validated. Use multiple datasets
if possible and compare your results with existing state-of-the-art methods.
ò 2.
Comparative Analysis Projects: I expect most of the projects
to fall into this category. These should involve implementing and evalu-
ating several generative models on a common problem. Use appropriate
evaluation metrics (beyond just accuracy), apply them across models and
datasets, and critically analyze which models perform best under which
conditions. Explain the mathematical foundations, experimental setup,
hyperparameter tuning, and rationale behind chosen configurations.
All submitted code must be well-organized, with meaningful comments,
proper class and function definitions, and a clear structure that makes the im-
plementation self-explanatory.
1.2
Paper Requirements:
The research paper must be prepared using Springer LNCS Template format.
Your report should include all the sections and subsections shown in the pro-
vided sample paper. It should clearly and technically describe each step of your
project, along with a proper presentation and discussion of the results.
1.3
Prompt Requirements:
We expect substantial use of generative AI models such as GPT, Gemini, Claude,
etc. to get help in your projects. Therefore, all students must submit a separate
file containing the prompts they used, demonstrating proper prompt engineer-
ing techniques. This component will carry its own marks and will be evaluated
accordingly.

Generative AI Project Paper Guidelines
Akhtar Jamil and Abdullah Saleem
Department of Computer Science, National University of Computer and Emerging
Sciences
akhtar.jamil@nu.edu.pk; i220882@nu.edu.pk
Abstract. The title should clearly reflect the problem being addressed
and the approach used. The abstract must be written as one compact
paragraph that briefly introduces the problem, describes the method or
models implemented, highlights the key results obtained, and states the
main findings or conclusions of the study.The abstract should briefly
summarize the contents of the paper in 150û250 words.
Keywords: Generative AI ╖ Voice AI ╖ Diffusion Models ╖ Transformers.
1
Introduction
The introduction should be written in two to three well-structured paragraphs.
It should begin with a general description of the problem domain, explaining
the motivation and importance of the research. This should be followed by a
discussion of the research gap or limitation in existing approaches. Finally, the
main contributions of the paper should be clearly stated in two to three concise
bullet points within the paragraph to highlight what has been achieved in this
work.
2
Literature Review
The literature review should include a discussion of eight to ten relevant and re-
cent research papers. It should be written in four to five paragraphs, where each
paragraph synthesizes related work rather than listing papers individually. Stu-
dents should describe the contributions of prior work, identify their limitations,
and clearly position their own work in relation to these studies.
3
Materials and Methods
3.1
Dataset and Dataset Preparation
This subsection should describe the dataset or datasets used in the study. Stu-
dents should explain the source of the data, provide examples of the data (such
as images or text samples where applicable), and describe all preprocessing steps
applied. It should also include a discussion of the data distribution and a table
summarizing the total number of samples, as well as the split between training,
validation, and testing sets.

2
A. Jamil and A. Saleem
3.2
Methodology
This subsection should describe the models used in the study. Students should ex-
plain the architecture and working mechanism of each model, along with relevant
mathematical formulations and key equations. The training procedure should
also be described, including loss functions, optimization techniques, and impor-
tant hyperparameters. If a novel method is proposed, its contribution should be
clearly highlighted and differentiated from existing approaches.
4
Experimental Results
4.1
Experimental Setup
This subsection should describe the complete experimental environment. Stu-
dents should include details about the hardware used (such as CPU, GPU, and
RAM specifications), the software frameworks and libraries used for implemen-
tation, and the execution environment (such as Google Colab or local systems).
The description should be sufficient to ensure reproducibility of the experiments.
4.2
Evaluation Metrics
This subsection should describe the evaluation metrics used to assess model
performance. Students should explain each metric in detail, justify why it is
appropriate for the problem, and include mathematical definitions or equations
where applicable.
4.3
Results and Analysis
This subsection should present all experimental results in a structured and com-
prehensive manner. Students must include plots such as training versus valida-
tion curves against epochs, error plots, and any other relevant visualizations.
Quantitative results should be presented in tables that allow direct compari-
son between models. Visual outputs, such as generated samples or predictions,
should also be included where applicable. In addition to presenting results, stu-
dents must provide a detailed analysis explaining the observed trends, comparing
model performance, discussing strengths and limitations, and highlighting key
insights derived from the experiments.
5
Conclusion
The conclusion should be written as a single paragraph summarizing the key
findings of the study. It should highlight the main contributions, identify the
best-performing model or approach, and briefly suggest possible directions for
future work.
