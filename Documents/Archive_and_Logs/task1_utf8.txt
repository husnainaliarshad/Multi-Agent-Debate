Project Proposal Report
Abstract. This paper presents a technical report on a localized, de-
centralized multi-agent debate (MAD) system designed to maximize the
reasoning efficacy of Small Language Models (SLMs) with fewer than 10
billion parameters. The system deploys three interacting 7B-parameter
agents in a structured Judge-Proposer-Critic topology to investigate whether
an emergent consensus generated by multiple SLMs can statistically
outperform the zero-shot reasoning of a single 70B-parameter frontier
model. The framework integrates ReAct tool-use paradigms and Ac-
tive Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) for factual grounding, and
is evaluated on the LegalBench-RAG benchmark. Targeted mitigation
strategies are proposed to address Self-Preferential Bias in the Judge
agent, hallucination loops stemming from ReAct syntax failure in SLMs,
and the complexity of evaluating dynamic retrieval pipelines.
Keywords: Small Language Models · Multi-Agent Debate · Retrieval-Augmented
Generation · ReAct · Legal Reasoning · Constrained Decoding
1
Research Goals
The primary goal of this research project is to maximize the reasoning efficacy of
Small Language Models (SLMs)—specifically those under 10 billion parameters,
such as 7B architectures—by deploying them in a highly structured, interactive
debate framework. Prior work has demonstrated that multi-agent orchestration
can substantially enhance SLM capabilities, with recent systems achieving com-
petitive performance against much larger models through collaborative deliber-
ation [6,1].
The study aims to quantitatively determine whether a synergistic Emer-
gent Consensus generated by three interacting 7B models can statistically out-
perform the zero-shot reasoning capabilities of a single 70B-parameter frontier
model. This hypothesis is grounded in evidence that multi-agent SLM systems
can potentially match or exceed LLM-level performance through collaborative
design [1,8]. Furthermore, the research seeks to empirically evaluate the com-
pounding effects of integrating ReAct tool-use paradigms and Active Retrieval-
Augmented Generation (RAG) as factual grounding mechanisms within this
multi-agent setting.

Project Description
The project proposes a localized, decentralized artificial intelligence orchestra-
tion designed for a resource-constrained environment—specifically, a dual-T4
GPU setup providing approximately 32GB of total VRAM, as available on plat-
forms such as Kaggle. It circumvents the financial and privacy costs associated
with cloud-based LLM APIs by utilizing aggressively quantized models (e.g.,
4-bit GGUF or AWQ formats) and efficient execution backends such as vLLM.
The core architecture is a Judge-Proposer-Critic multi-agent debate. The
Proposer initiates an argument or solution, the Critic adversarially attacks the
logic or retrieves counter-evidence, and a single Judge model evaluates the full
debate trajectory to declare a final verdict. This design is motivated by the
empirical success of structured debate settings in NLP tasks: the MAD frame-
work introduced by Liang et al. [4] showed that a “tit-for-tat” argumentation
process between agents—managed by a judge—leads to more reliable solutions
than single-model self-reflection. More recently, multi-agent debate has been ap-
plied to tasks as diverse as fake news detection [5] and domain-specific RAG
chunking [9], confirming the generality of the paradigm.
The system operates on a zero-shot/few-shot basis, relying on strict prompt
engineering, constrained token sampling, and dynamic tool orchestration, with-
out any model fine-tuning.
3
Methodology
Because the models will not undergo fine-tuning, the methodology relies heavily
on inference-time orchestration and strict execution protocols.
Zero-Shot/Few-Shot Prompt Injection. Agents are assigned strict personas via
system prompts, utilizing 3–5 “golden” trajectory examples to enforce the IRAC
(Issue, Rule, Application, Conclusion) legal reasoning format. This few-shot
strategy follows the design principles validated in SLM function-calling stud-
ies, where structured prompt injection is critical for output quality [3].
Sequential Orchestration. The debate follows a sequential pipeline managed
by an orchestrator (e.g., LangGraph). The Proposer generates a structured re-
sponse, which is passed to the Critic. The sequential model mirrors debate flow
designs used in multi-agent systems for creative and reasoning tasks [7,8].
Constrained Sampling. To enforce structural fidelity during tool use, token gen-
eration is strictly bound by context-free grammar parsers (e.g., Guidance or
Outlines), ensuring syntactically valid JSON/YAML outputs. This constraint is
directly motivated by empirical findings that SLMs in zero-shot settings achieve
JSON-parsable success rates below 10% [3], making compiler-level enforcement
a necessity rather than an optimisation.

Mathematical Adaptive Stopping. To prevent the debate from running indef-
initely or devolving into unproductive consensus, the orchestrator employs a

mechanism is inspired by the finding that the “adaptive break of debate” is a
critical hyperparameter for MAD performance [4].
4
Literature Review
The theoretical foundation of this project intersects multi-agent dynamics, re-
trieval mechanisms, and constrained text generation.
4.1
Multi-Agent Debate and Emergent Consensus
MAD frameworks have been proven to mitigate the Degeneration of Thought
(DoT) problem, in which a single model becomes overconfident in a flawed rea-
soning path and fails to self-correct through reflection [4]. When properly orches-
trated, a debate-driven architecture encourages divergent thinking and leads to
consensus outputs that carry synergistic predictive information exceeding the
capacity of any individual agent.
Recent empirical work confirms the breadth of this effect. In the domain of
misinformation detection, the TruEDebate (TED) system demonstrated that a
structured multi-agent debate process—comprising opening statements, cross-
examination, rebuttal, and closing statements—substantially surpasses tradi-
tional classification baselines in both accuracy and interpretability [5]. For text
chunking in domain-specific RAG, QChunker employs a four-agent debate pipeline
(question outline generator, text segmenter, integrity reviewer, and knowledge
completer) to produce semantically coherent chunks that improve downstream
retrieval quality [9].
For the specific challenge of using SLMs as judges, JudgeBoard proposes the
Multi-Agent Judging (MAJ) framework, in which multiple SLMs with distinct
reasoning profiles collaborate to approximate LLM-level judgment accuracy. No-
tably, MAJ using smaller backbone models performs comparably to, or better
than, their larger counterparts on the MATH benchmark [1]. Complementarily,
Wei et al. [7] demonstrate that a principle-guided LLM-as-a-Judge with multi-
agent reward shaping can unlock creative capabilities in 7B-parameter SLMs,
illustrating that judgment and generation quality are jointly improvable through
multi-agent feedback.
4.2
SLM Orchestration and Structural Fragility
Beyond debate, recent work addresses how multiple SLMs can be systematically
orchestrated. SLM-MUX [6] introduces a three-stage approach—model selection,
architecture design, and test-time scaling—to coordinate complementary SLMs,

achieving up to 13.4% improvement on MATH over existing orchestration meth-
ods. This demonstrates that the complementarity of different small models is a
resource that structured orchestration can exploit effectively.
ResilioMate [8] offers a resilient multi-agent framework that addresses both

At the same time, empirical studies such as Small Models, Big Tasks [3]
demonstrate that SLMs operating in zero-shot settings struggle immensely with
function calling, frequently achieving JSON-parsable success rates below 10%.
This highlights the absolute necessity of token-level constrained sampling to
ensure loop stability.
4.3
Active vs. Passive Retrieval-Augmented Generation
Traditional (Passive) RAG retrieves information once before generation begins,
leaving the system prone to context bloat and noise distraction if the initial
retrieval is poorly calibrated. Advanced frameworks introduce Active RAG, in
which models iteratively anticipate future content needs and retrieve documents
on demand based on low-confidence tokens.
Decoupling the search agent from the generator and training it with a Gain
Beyond RAG (GBR) reward—quantifying the improvement in generation ac-
curacy over a naive RAG baseline—has shown superior efficiency in multi-turn
environments. The s3 framework [2] demonstrates that a lightweight, model-
agnostic searcher trained with this GBR objective requires only 2,400 training
samples to consistently outperform baselines trained on 70× more data, across
both general and medical QA benchmarks. The multi-agent architecture pro-
posed in this project adopts this GBR principle, assigning the Active RAG role
adversarially to both Proposer and Critic agents.
5
Dataset: LegalBench-RAG
The project will be evaluated using the LegalBench-RAG dataset, the first
benchmark specifically designed to evaluate the precise retrieval step of RAG
pipelines within the legal domain. It consists of 6,858 human-annotated query-
answer pairs spanning a corpus of over 79 million characters, covering diverse
legal documents including NDAs, M&A agreements, commercial contracts, and
privacy policies.
Unlike generative-focused benchmarks, LegalBench-RAG focuses on extract-
ing minimal, highly relevant text segments rather than large, imprecise chunks.
This prevents context window bloat, reduces inference latency, and mitigates
the risk of models losing track of the prompt during extended legal reasoning—a
concern directly analogous to the semantic fragmentation challenge addressed in
domain-specific RAG systems [9].

Highlighted Issues and Mitigation Strategies
7.1
Self-Preferential Bias in the Judge Agent
The Issue. When a single LLM acts as an adjudicator, it exhibits a pronounced
Self-Preference Bias (SPB), systematically favouring arguments that structurally
or stylistically align with its own pre-trained generative patterns, regardless of
objective accuracy. This is a well-documented failure mode in LLM-as-a-Judge
frameworks, and is particularly acute when the judge and the agents share the
same model family [1,7].
The Mitigation. To support fair adjudication in a zero-shot setting, the system
will apply black-box perturbations (e.g., synonym replacement) to the Proposer’s
and Critic’s outputs to obfuscate authorship. Furthermore, the Judge’s task will
employ cognitive load decomposition, asking it to rate specific IRAC elements
individually rather than issuing a single holistic score. This decomposition strat-
egy mirrors the fine-grained, criterion-aware evaluation principles proposed in
JudgeBoard [1].

7.2
Hallucination Loops and ReAct Syntax Failure
The Issue. SLMs notoriously fail to maintain the strict syntax required for ReAct
(Thought-Action-Observation), causing the orchestrator to crash. Additionally,
agents can fall into a Hallucination Loop, in which they hallucinate a tool output,
validate their own hallucination, and confidently present a fabricated conclusion.
Empirical studies confirm that this is a systemic weakness of SLMs in zero-shot
function-calling scenarios [3].
The Mitigation. Strict schema enforcement via constrained sampling (Guid-
ance/Outlines) guarantees that the SLM can only generate syntactically valid
JSON/YAML. Invalid tokens are rejected at the compiler level before execution,
physically preventing syntax-based hallucination loops. This approach is comple-
mented by the resilient task-execution strategies developed in frameworks such
as ResilioMate [8], which demonstrate the value of structured error-interception
in SLM multi-agent pipelines.
7.3
Evaluating Active RAG and ReAct Tool Usage
The Issue. Evaluating a dynamic system requires moving beyond simple exact-
match correctness to evaluating the process of retrieval and reasoning across
multiple debate turns.
The Mitigation. Two complementary evaluation metrics are adopted.
– Active RAG Evaluation: Measured using the Gain Beyond RAG (GBR)
metric [2], which quantifies the improvement in downstream accuracy specif-
ically attributable to dynamic retrieval over a passive baseline. Search effi-
ciency will also be tracked by penalising both over-searching and under-
searching.
– ReAct Evaluation: Measured via Turn Faithfulness—ensuring tool out-
puts strictly ground the agent’s claims—and Turn Contextual Precision,
evaluating the relevance of retrieved legal statutes at each debate turn.
8
System Features
The final engineered system will encapsulate the following six core features:
1. Judge-Proposer-Critic Multi-Agent Topology: Three distinct zero-
shot agents orchestrated sequentially to debate legal queries, following the
MAD paradigm of [4].
2. LegalBench-RAG Integration: A robust testing pipeline utilising a spe-
cialised 79M-character legal retrieval corpus.
3. Grammar-Constrained Decoding: Real-time token masking to enforce
strict JSON ReAct syntax for tool execution, addressing the format-adherence
failures documented in SLM function-calling studies [3].

4. Adversarial Active RAG: Dynamic tool-calling where the Critic and Pro-
poser autonomously trigger vector database searches to ground their argu-
ments or invalidate peers’, building on the GBR-trained search paradigm
of [2] and the domain RAG advances of [9].
5. Adaptive Stability Detection: A mathematically driven stopping mech-
anism based on Kolmogorov-Smirnov testing over a Beta-Binomial distribu-
tion, to terminate debates optimally before social herding occurs [4].
6. SPB-Mitigated Adjudication: Obfuscation and cognitive decomposition
prompting strategies to ensure the single 7B Judge remains impartial, in-
formed by best practices from SLM judgment benchmarking [1,7].

Conclusion
This technical report has outlined a principled framework for scaling up the rea-
soning capabilities of Small Language Models through structured multi-agent
debate, without incurring the computational and financial costs of large fron-
tier models. By combining a Judge-Proposer-Critic topology with grammar-
constrained decoding, adversarial Active RAG, and a mathematically grounded
stopping criterion, the proposed system addresses the key failure modes of SLMs
in zero-shot legal reasoning tasks. The framework synthesises a rich body of
recent work on multi-agent orchestration [4,1,6,8,5,9], SLM structural limita-
tions [3,7], and model-agnostic search optimisation [2]. Evaluation on LegalBench-
RAG will provide a rigorous empirical test of whether emergent multi-agent
consensus in small models can challenge the zero-shot supremacy of much larger
parameter counts.
